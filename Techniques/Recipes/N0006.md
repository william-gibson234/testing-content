Suppose we are given the PDF $f_X(x)$ of a continuous random variable $X$, and we want the PDF $f_{g(X)}$ of the derived variable given by composing $X$ with $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$.

The PDF of $g(X)$ is *not* (usually) equal to $g\circ f_X(x)$.

If we know $f_X(x)$, we can find $f_{g(X)}(x)$ using a 3-step process:

**(1) Integrate PDF to get CDF:**

$$
F_X(x) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^x f_X(t)\,dt
$$

**(2) Find $F_{g(X)}$, the CDF of $g(X)$, by comparing conditions:**

When $g$ is *monotone increasing*, we have equivalent conditions:

$$
\begin{align*}
g(X)\leq x\quad&\!\!\!\iff\quad X\leq g^{-1}(x)\\\\
\gg\gg \qquad P[\,g(X)\leq x\,]\quad&=\quad P[\,X\leq g^{-1}(x)\,]\\\\
\gg\gg \qquad F_{g(X)}(x)\quad&=\quad F_X(g^{-1}(x))
\end{align*}
$$

**(3) Differentiate CDF to recover PDF:**

$$
\begin{gather*}
f_{g(X)}(x)\;=\; \frac{d}{dx}F_{g(X)}(x) \quad \gg\gg \quad \frac{d}{dx}F_X(g^{-1}(x))\\\\
\gg\gg \quad f_X(g^{-1}(x))\cdot \frac{d}{dx}(g^{-1})
\end{gather*}
$$
