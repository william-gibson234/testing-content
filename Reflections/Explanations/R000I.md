For example, if $L\sim\mathrm{Pasc}(3,\,0.25)$, then $P_L(8)$ gives the probability of getting: the $3^\text{rd}$ success on (precisely) the $8^\text{th}$ trial. Interpret the formula: the number of ways to arrange $2$ successes among $7$ prior trials, times the probability of exactly $3$ successes and $5$ failures in one specific sequence.
