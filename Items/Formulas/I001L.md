For a Bernoulli process of $n$ trials with $P[T]=p$ and $P[F]=q=1-p$, the probability of exactly $k$ successes is:

$$
P[S=k] \;=\; {n\choose k}\cdot p^{k}\cdot q^{n-k}
$$

Each outcome with exactly $k$ successes and $n-k$ failures has probability $p^{k}q^{n-k}$. The *number* of such outcomes is the number of ways to choose $k$ of the $n$ trials to be successes, ${n\choose k}$.
