The binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{k}$ counts the number of ways to choose $k$ objects from $n$ distinct objects and equals $\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$. An equivalent and useful form is the falling factorial expansion: $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2) \cdots (n-k+1)}{k!}$, where the numerator is the product of $k$ consecutive integers starting from $n$ and decreasing. This form is particularly useful when analyzing limiting behavior as $n$ becomes very large.
