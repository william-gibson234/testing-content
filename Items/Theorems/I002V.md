The Poisson distribution is the limit of binomial distributions as $n\to\infty$ while $np$ remains fixed, so $p\to 0$ in perfect balance with $n\to\infty$.

Fix $\alpha$ and define $p_n=\alpha/n$. Let $X_n\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\,p_n)$ and let $Y_\alpha\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\alpha)$. Then for any $k\in\mathbb{N}$:

$$
P_{X_n}(k) \to P_{Y_\alpha}(k) \quad \text{as }\, n\to\infty
$$
