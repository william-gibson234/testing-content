Consider a sequence of increasing $n$ with decreasing $p$ such that $\alpha=np$ is held fixed. For example, let $n=1,2,3,\dots$ while $p=\frac{\alpha}{n}$.

Think of this process as increasing the number of causal agents represented: group the agents together into $n$ bunches, and consider the odds that such a bunch activates. (For a call center, a bunch is a group of users; for radioactive decay, a bunch is a unit of mass of Uranium atoms.)

As $n$ doubles, the number of agents per bunch drops by half. (For the call center, a group is divided in half, so there are twice as many groups but half the odds of one group making a call; for the Uranium, a chunk of mass is divided in half, giving twice as many portions with half the odds of a decay occurring in each portion.)

This process is formally called *division of a distribution*.
